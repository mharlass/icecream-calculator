# Cardamom & Lime Ice Cream

**Tested and confirmed good.** One Ninja Creami Deluxe tub (~700 g). Eggless, gelatin +
xanthan stabilized, cooked base. Built on the Underbelly formulation method and balanced for
a machine that shaves a frozen puck rather than churning a liquid.

## Composition

| Metric | This recipe | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Milk fat | 13.4% | ≤15% |
| MSNF | 11.5% | 10–12% |
| Added sugars | 12.2% | 11–14% |
| Total nonfat solids | 24.5% | 22–25% |
| Total solids | 37.9% | 37–42% |
| Water | 62.1% | 58–63% |
| Stabilizer | 0.23% | 0.15–0.25% |
| **POD** (sweetness) | **117** | 110–120 |
| **PAC** (softness) | **252** | 245–255 for a Creami |

Dextrose is 44% of the sugar blend, under the 50% ceiling. That's deliberate: it buys the
freezing-point depression a frozen puck needs without making the ice cream sweet.

## Ingredients

**Dairy and dry**

| | |
|---|---|
| Whole milk (3.5%) | 306 g |
| Cream (35%) | 239 g |
| Nonfat dry milk powder | 40 g |
| Sucrose | 48 g |
| Dextrose | 38 g |
| Fine salt | 0.9 g |

**Stabilizers**

| | |
|---|---|
| Gelatin | 1.2 g |
| Xanthan gum | 0.4 g |

**Flavor**

| | |
|---|---|
| Green cardamom pods | 4 g (about 18–20 pods) |
| Lime zest | from 3 limes, green only, no pith |
| Fresh lime juice, strained | 30 g |

Weigh gelatin and xanthan on a scale reading to 0.01 g. **Weigh gelatin, never count sheets**
— sheet weight varies by grade, because sheets are cut to equal gelling power, not equal mass.

**Dr. Oetker Blatt Gelatine weiß:** 1.67 g per sheet (6 per 10 g pack), Gold Extra, roughly
220–240 bloom. Use **1.2 g, about ¾ of one sheet**, cut with scissors. Go to 1.1 g for a
softer, less set body.

Other grades matched on bloom-grams (weight × bloom, target ≈ 250): bronze ~2.0 g,
silver ~1.6 g, platinum ~1.0 g. Powdered gelatin at 200–225 bloom: 1.2 g.

## Method

**1. Zest into the sugar.** Zest the limes directly onto the sucrose and rub with your
fingertips for a minute until the sugar is damp, green-flecked and intensely fragrant. The
sugar crystals abrade the zest and pull the oils out far better than steeping does. Then add
the dextrose, milk powder, xanthan and salt and whisk thoroughly. Mixing the powders together
first is what stops the xanthan clumping: an ingredient only clumps with itself.

**2. Bloom the gelatin.** *Sheets:* soak in a bowl of plenty of cold water for 5–10 minutes,
then lift out and squeeze firmly. Cold water only — warm water starts dissolving the sheet and
you lose gelatin with the discarded soaking water. Don't soak sheets in milk; they need free
water to hydrate and you need to squeeze the excess back off. *Powder:* bloom in 10 g taken
from the measured milk and add cup and all at step 7.

**3. Toast and crush the cardamom.** Dry pan, medium heat, 60–90 seconds until fragrant. Cool,
then crack the pods open under a heavy pan. Don't grind to powder.

**4. Blend cold.** Milk and cream into the blender (if you bloomed *powdered* gelatin in milk,
hold back that 10 g). Start at the lowest speed that forms a vortex, rain in the dry mix, then
blend high for 30 seconds.

**5. Cook, 75 °C for 45 minutes,** with the crushed cardamom added. Cover the pot, or better,
seal it in a bag in a water bath. Stir occasionally. This step earns its keep: it hydrates
everything, and it partially denatures the whey proteins so they act as both emulsifier and
stabilizer. With no egg and no lecithin here, that protein work is doing real structural jobs,
so don't shorten it.

**6. Strain out the cardamom,** pressing the pods to recover the mix.

**7. Cool to 65 °C, then stir in the bloomed gelatin** (squeezed sheet, or the bloomed powder
and its milk) until fully dissolved. Add it after the cook, not before: 45 minutes at 75 °C
would hydrolyze some of the gelatin and cost gel strength.

**8. Homogenize hot.** Blend at the highest speed for a full minute while the fat is still
liquid. This is what makes it smooth.

**9. Chill fast** to under 5 °C in an ice bath. Don't let it coast down in the fridge.

**10. Add the lime juice cold, last, and slowly** — blender on low, poured in a thin stream.
Then stop.

**11. Weigh and top up to 700 g** with cold milk if the cook lost water to evaporation.

**12. Age 4–12 hours** in the fridge below 4 °C. It will set to a soft gel.

**13. Break the gel** with a 10-second blend, pour into the Deluxe tub to the MAX FILL line,
level the surface flat, and freeze flat for at least 24 hours at −18 °C or colder.

**14. Spin on Lite Ice Cream.** Powdery → Re-spin with 1–2 tsp milk. Soupy → refreeze 20–30 min.

## When each ingredient goes in

| # | Moment | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start | Lime zest + sucrose, rubbed together |
| 2 | Straight after | Dextrose, milk powder, xanthan, salt → into the zested sugar |
| 3 | In parallel | Gelatin blooming in cold water, set aside |
| 4 | Before heating | Milk + cream + the dry mix → blender |
| 5 | Into the cook | Cardamom (removed again at step 6) |
| 6 | After the cook, 65 °C | The bloomed gelatin |
| 7 | After chilling below 5 °C | Lime juice. Nothing goes in after this. |

Cardamom is the only ingredient that comes back out.

---

## Why the lime goes in cold and last

**Casein is the real hazard.** Milk protein precipitates at pH 4.6; fresh milk sits at 6.7,
lime juice at 2.0–2.4. Heat and concentration both make it worse — hot milk plus acid is how
paneer gets made. At 30 g in 700 g you add roughly 0.2% citric acid, landing near pH 5.6–6.0,
a comfortable margin. The whey proteins denatured in step 5 are also *more* acid-sensitive
unfolded than native, which reinforces the same ordering.

**Gelatin loses strength below pH 5,** and heating gelatin in acid hydrolyzes it permanently.
Gelatin in first, lime after, no heat once the lime is in.

**Xanthan is completely unbothered** — exceptionally acid-stable across a wide pH range. It's
arguably the ideal gum for a citrus base.

**Acid also suppresses perceived sweetness,** which is why this recipe sits at POD 117 near the
top of the range rather than the 112 an unflavored base would use.

**Zest carries no acid.** For more lime aroma, add zest, never juice. Past about 6% juice (42 g
here) the curdling risk climbs sharply for little flavor gain.

## Adjustments

| Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too hard, powdery, needs several Re-spins | Less sucrose, more dextrose. Holds sweetness, raises PAC. |
| Soupy straight out of the machine | Less dextrose, more sucrose. Or just refreeze 20–30 min. |
| Right texture, too sweet | Less sucrose, more dextrose. Holds PAC, drops POD. |
| Right texture, not sweet enough | More sucrose, less dextrose. Holds PAC, raises POD. |
| Thin body, watery melt | Raise xanthan toward 0.6 g. Xanthan governs melted texture. |
| Chewy, gummy, too much set | Drop gelatin toward 1.0 g. Gelatin governs frozen body. |
| Grainy or curdled after the juice | pH went too low. Less juice, more zest, added colder and slower. |
| Slightly sandy on the tongue | Lactose crystallizing. Drop milk powder by 5 g. |

**Judge sweetness warm, not cold.** Cold suppresses sweetness perception, and frozen suppresses
it further. Warm a teaspoon of the finished base in your hand before deciding — it should taste
a touch too sweet at room temperature to land right frozen.

**Total solids sit at 37.9%,** the low end of the ideal band, because the lime brings ~27 g of
water. With inulin on hand, this variant lifts solids to about 39% at the same POD and PAC and
will be noticeably creamier:

> milk 293 g · cream 241 g · milk powder 41 g · sucrose 51 g · dextrose 31 g · **inulin 14 g**
> · lime juice 30 g · gelatin 1.2 g · xanthan 0.4 g · salt 0.9 g

Soy lecithin is optional rather than merely nice to have, because the 45-minute cook already
generates emulsifier from the whey proteins. If you want it, 1.4 g goes in with the dry
ingredients.
