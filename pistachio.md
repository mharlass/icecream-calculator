# Pistachio Ice Cream

**Untested — balanced, not yet made.** One Ninja Creami Deluxe tub (~700 g). Eggless, cooked
base, stabilized with CMC, guar and a trace of kappa carrageenan, emulsified with lecithin.
Built with the calculator on the same method as the tested [cardamom & lime](cardamom-lime.md)
recipe, and rebalanced around a nut paste that is nearly half fat.

Flavor source: **100% pistachio paste**, Albert Heijn, label per 100 g — energy 608 kcal,
fat 48 g (6.8 g saturated), carbohydrate 20 g of which sugars 18 g, fibre 6.0 g, protein 21 g,
salt 0 g. Modeled as fat 48 · sucrose 18 · fibre 6 · other solids 25 · water 3.

| Total solids | 41.0% | 37–42% |
| Water | 59.1% | 58–63% |
| Stabilizer | 0.18% | 0.15–0.20% |
| **POD** (sweetness) | **114** | 110–120 |
| **PAC** (softness) | **249** | 245–255 for a Creami |

Dextrose is 44% of the sugar blend, under the 50% ceiling. Estimated lactose sits at 8.8% of
the water phase, under the 10% sandiness line.

**Nonfat solids are 2.5 points above the window on purpose.** 90 g of paste brings 27 g of
pistachio protein and fibre that no dairy formula has to carry. Cutting milk solids far enough
to land inside 22–25% would strip the mix of the protein that stabilizes the emulsion. Total
solids still land at 41%, inside the range that actually governs body, so that is the number
being held.

## Method

**1. Mix the powders.** Sucrose, dextrose, inulin, milk powder, CMC, guar, kappa carrageenan
and salt into one bowl and whisk thoroughly. Preblending is what stops the gums clumping: an
ingredient only clumps with itself.

**2. Blend cold.** Milk and cream into the blender. Lowest speed that forms a vortex, rain in
the dry mix, then 30 seconds on high.

**3. Cook, 75–80 °C for 45 minutes.** Covered pot, or sealed in a bag in a water bath. Stir
occasionally. This hydrates the guar and CMC, dissolves the kappa carrageenan (which needs
roughly 70–80 °C — check your supplier's sheet), and partially denatures the whey proteins so
they work as emulsifier and stabilizer.

**The pistachio paste does not go into the cook.** Forty-five minutes of heat and oxygen dulls
the green and flattens the roast aroma for no functional gain.

**4. Cool to about 65 °C.** Meanwhile whisk the lecithin into the pistachio paste until smooth
— it disperses far better into fat than into water.

**5. Add the paste and homogenize hot.** Scrape the lecithin-loaded paste into the warm base
and blend at the highest speed for a full minute. This step is the recipe. Pistachio fat
arrives as an intact nut emulsion, not as free oil ready to be structured, and a full minute of
shear while everything is liquid is what keeps it dispersed instead of separating into a greasy
layer at the top of the tub.

**6. Chill fast** to under 5 °C in an ice bath. Don't let it coast down in the fridge.

**7. Weigh and top up to 700 g** with cold milk to replace what the cook evaporated.

**8. Age 4–12 hours** below 4 °C. The kappa carrageenan will set it to a soft, sliceable gel.
That is correct.

**9. Break the gel** with a 10-second blend, pour into the Deluxe tub to the MAX FILL line,
level the surface, and freeze flat at least 24 hours at −18 °C or colder.

**10. Spin on Lite Ice Cream.** Powdery → Re-spin with 1–2 tsp milk. Soupy → refreeze 20–30 min.

## When each ingredient goes in

| # | Moment | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Start | Sucrose, dextrose, inulin, milk powder, CMC, guar, kappa, salt — whisked together dry |
| 2 | Before heating | Milk + cream + the dry mix → blender |
| 3 | Nothing | The cook runs on dairy alone |
| 4 | After the cook, 65 °C | Pistachio paste with the lecithin already whisked into it |
| 5 | After chilling below 5 °C | Nothing. Optional flavorings only. |

Nothing is strained out. Every gram of the paste stays in.

## Why this build

**Two fats, only one of which builds structure.** Pistachio fat is about 90% unsaturated and
stays liquid well below freezer temperature, so it never crystallizes and cannot take part in
the partial coalescence that gives churned ice cream its dry, scoopable body. It reads as
richness and mouthcoat, nothing else. That is why milk fat is held at 7.3% rather than pushed
to near zero: something has to actually crystallize. It is also why the lecithin is not
optional here, unlike in the cardamom recipe where the cook alone did the emulsifier work.

**Kappa instead of gelatin.** Kappa carrageenan gels with the calcium already in milk, so 0.15 g
does the frozen-body job the gelatin did, and it also holds the nut solids in suspension during
the long chill instead of letting them sink. It is a small dose for a reason — kappa in dairy
is strong, and overdosing it gives a brittle, short, sliceable texture rather than a creamy one.

**CMC and guar together** cover storage recrystallization and body, both hydrate without
needing locust bean gum's temperature, and the 45-minute hold gives the guar plenty of time to
reach full viscosity.

**Xanthan is being held in reserve.** The mix is already thick with nut solids, and xanthan's
rigid network is the one polysaccharide reported to read as gritty and colder in low-fat
sensory work. Add 0.1 g only if the melt turns out thin.

**Salt at 0.2%** is up from 0.13% in the cardamom recipe. Roasted nut flavor needs it, and it
is the cheapest PAC in the formula.

## The one number worth checking

The label says 18 g sugars per 100 g. USDA's reference for pistachios is 7.7 g. The label is
what this recipe is balanced on, but if it is a labeling artifact rather than a real value, the
mix lands at **POD 100 and PAC 236** — noticeably less sweet and distinctly harder out of the
machine.

The taste test decides it, warm, before freezing. If the cold base tastes flat rather than a
touch too sweet, rebalance to:

> milk 391 g · cream 103 g · milk powder 28 g · **sucrose 40 g** · **dextrose 38 g** · inulin 6 g
> · paste 90 g · lecithin 1.5 g · salt 1.4 g · CMC 0.6 g · guar 0.5 g · kappa 0.15 g

which returns POD 114 and PAC 250 against the lower sugar figure.

## Adjustments

| Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too hard, powdery, needs several Re-spins | Less sucrose, more dextrose. Holds sweetness, raises PAC. |
| Soupy straight out of the machine | Less dextrose, more sucrose. Or refreeze 20–30 min. |
| Right texture, too sweet | Less sucrose, more dextrose. |
| Not enough pistachio | Push the paste to 120 g: milk 408 g · cream 74 g · milk powder 30 g · sucrose 24 g · dextrose 40 g · **no inulin** · lecithin 1.8 g, rest unchanged. Fat 14.0%, solids 42.2%, POD 114, PAC 250. |
| Greasy, or an oily film on the tub | Under-homogenized. Blend longer at step 5, and hotter. Or raise lecithin toward 2.1 g (0.3%). |
| Brittle, short, cuts rather than scoops | Too much kappa. Drop to 0.10 g. |
| Thin, watery melt | Raise CMC toward 0.8 g, or add 0.1 g xanthan. |
| Gummy, elastic, slow to release flavor | Total gums too high. Cut guar first. |
| Slightly sandy on the tongue | Lactose crystallizing. Drop milk powder by 5 g. |

**Judge sweetness warm.** Cold suppresses sweetness and frozen suppresses it further. Warm a
teaspoon of the finished base in your hand — it should taste a touch too sweet at room
temperature to land right frozen.

**Color will be beige-green, not neon.** Nothing here corrects for that, and nothing should.
Keeping the paste out of the cook is the only lever this recipe pulls.

**Optional, added cold at step 7:** 2–3 g orange blossom water, or one drop of bitter almond
extract. Both are traditional with pistachio and both are easy to overdo — measure, don't pour.
